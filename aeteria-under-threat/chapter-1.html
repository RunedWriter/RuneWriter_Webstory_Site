    For Aeteria it is the year 312 of the Unified Era, two decades have passed since the war against the Symbiants began. Now at last there is potential for an ally in the grand conflict. The void-born tribes as they call themselves are a confederation of refugees and survivors of past Symbiant expansions. A representative of their predominant species, the Cuthians, makes the perilous journey to the new power so that he may understand the truth of these strange newcomers. Will these 'Humans' prove to be a breath of hope? Or will the militant upstarts prove more a poison than a cure? Follow attentively as we experience Tyr Iffyn's wild and rewarding mission of bonding with the Humans of Aeteria.

    Arrival on Aeteria.

    The journey through Aeterian space was as pleasantly uneventful and brief as reaching it had been hazardous and long. The Symbiant blockade utilized dimensional anchors to disrupt all forms of warp or void travel. This meant we first had to arrive outside of its range, send word to Aeteria, and wait while stealthed for the Aeterian assault to disrupt the blockade and allow our entry. That did not include the stressful journey through Symbiant space just to reach the border. One filled with patrol dodging and scavenging for scraps on the outskirts of systems like a destitute junker.

    The battle itself was a remarkable spectacle, the Aeterians certainly live up to their militant reputation. Close range fighters emerged directly out of void rifts in between Symbiant formations, firing their payloads at recklessly close ranges before disappearing from real space as quickly as they'd come. Waves of these fighters shattered the symbiant battle formations before the assault fleet came into range to bombard the capital ships in earnest.

    The chaos provided ample disruption for us to rendezvous with our would be escorts. We boarded just as Symbiant reinforcements began arriving en masse. Too late to aid in the battle as the human fleet withdrew in short order behind a screen of seeker mines.

    "One last parting gift for the mind-thralled [redacted]" I was told after inquiring about the seeker mines, a quote I included in my notes, along with the astoundingly light casualties suffered. Our systems indicated the Symbiants suffered ten fold the losses of the battle in personnel alone, with ship and component losses looking to be even more lopsided. It was true the Symbiant weren't known for their militancy, tending to overwhelm with numbers those they couldn't infest directly, but such a discrepancy in losses was something the coalition had been hard pressed to believe as more than rumor. The thought of their potential made me shudder. I once again wondered if this warrior race could truly be trusted with the technology we were bringing as incentive.

    With our final hurdle accomplished the last leg of the journey couldn't have been more peaceful. Being a relatively small dominion within the galaxy, the Aeterian borders were not large enough to merit stops for resupply. After arriving at the first mobile defense titan (a combination of battleship and space station), we swapped escorts and went directly to Earth. I spent the two day journey familiarizing myself with human customs and confirming our present understanding of Human societal structure. Please reference my secondary annotations if interested.

    The human homeworld of Earth turned out to be a blue-green pearl, gleaming beautifully from orbit as we descended into atmosphere. After being in artificial gravity for so long, it was pleasantly surreal to feel the weight of a planetary body's pull once more. The decent through orbit was as long as expected, and so I took joy in observing the landscape below me. It was stunning.

    That was not to say the stations and space habs of the coalition were dreary. Every station and ship of the coalition was personalized to represent it's clan or owner of course, but even with all the soul and passion we put into our cabins and ship exteriors, it couldn't rival the beauty and identity that came from an entire planet and it's history of life.

    The ache of memory fell upon me as I saw the beauty of the stretching horizon. Visions of floating forests and crystal homes now found only in my mind. A homeworld long lost. I shook the vestiges of pain and rage from my mind, the looming figure of our destination was coming into view, and duty waits as impatiently as time for such personal distractions.

    For the throne of a multi-system dominion, the Aeterian palace was grander and more modest than I'd anticipated. Built around a space elevator it was a city unto itself towering up from the ground like a mountain. Landing ports, logistic hubs, residential blocks and garrisons all interwoven in its architectural tapestry. The palaced seemed to lack for nothing, save perhaps regality. It was most certainly beautiful, but it was the beauty of individuals allowed to design their own, not the organized majesty of a palace. There were many grand monuments at least, but all of those appeared dedicated as either memorials, grand art of a general nature or natural displays of art, like a climbing forest that stretched a thousand meters high. I saw nothing depicting the grandeur of a ruler or individual.

    The central palace, nestled like a gem midway up the tower had landing pads spaced out in abundance around it. Our transport was guided to one in front of a large, obsidian-walled entrance where A group of soldiers and representatives awaited me. After a brief exchange of courtesy introductions and a redundant round of security clearances, we proceeded inside. Past the reception into a quietly majestic conference hall.

    Like the entrance, the hall was obsidian walled. A redwood table, polished and gleaming, rested in the center. Four humans were waiting for me, one standing out starkly among them. Starting from the left was a woman in uniform, the air around her rippling and crackling with raw magical energy. I recognized her as Lillian Dawnstar. Next was a tired looking figure dressed in a finely tailored suit with eyes that glinted with a hidden sharpness. I believe her name was Asha Void, the 'voice of the Aeter'. Beside her was a jovial looking man in what I was to understand was stylish attire, fingers a-blur over their communicator as they watched my entry with rapt attention. I recognized him as Andrew Rayleigh, head of Aeterian commerce. Finally, the central figure of the room looked young, or at least young compared to those around him. I felt warmth and frost in grey-blue eyes that pierced me. He was the most modestly dressed, in a official's uniform, but something about him left me with no doubt that this was the Aeter.

    As I entered, I was announced by the soldiers at the door.

    "Representative of the Void-born Tribes, 'Weaver of Knowledge' Tyr Iffyn and company."

    "Earth, Aeteria and our people welcome you Tyr Iffyn. I trust your journey through our space proved uneventful" The young man I suspected to be the Aeter said. His voice seemed to resonate... differently than I expected. Deeper, and more impactful than his physical frame would have implied.

    "It was remarkably so. A pleasant change from my journey to your borders. I am... impressed your interior remains so at peace after enduring such a protracted conflict with our mutual enemy. War may not be their specialty, but they do not lack for supplies, personnel, or firepower. In truth, when we first heard the rumors of the conflict, we expected and made ready to welcome refugees. I am elated that it never came to be, and am sorrowful for the delay in this meeting that expectation caused."

    "It is my understanding that the Void Born Coalition exists nomadically. It is unsurprising that you would want to ensure you do not casually send your support to a losing or unknown element. It is regretful this meeting needed two decades of conflict to serve a foundation of trust. However you are here now, and I am happy to finally encounter potential friends in this vast, seemingly hostile universe. Please, take a seat and make yourself comfortable."

    I did so, the chair offered having been designed perfectly for a Cuthulian frame. A pleasant surprise I hadn't expected for our first meeting, as they'd only had our biological specifications for a month. The project of expanding Symbiant detection by sharing our scanner technologies with each other having been completed in advance of this meeting. That left the most pressing matter to be establishing a favorable first impression.

    "Your care and consideration to hospitality honors me and does your people credit. There are many things to discuss in preparation of future cooperation. To begin with, and I do apologize for this, but even if we all know it's nothing more than Symbiant propaganda there is widespread accusation of forced relocations, conscription, inducting fanaticism into your warriors, and cruel forced labor conditions born by your population. The coalition made it a priority for me to confirm the truth of the situation before allowing me to proceed into deeper talks of cooperation."

    The air around the table grew tense, as I expected. I did not expect the response I was to receive, however.

    "It depends on how you look at it, but if you are asking us to disprove of those rumors entirely. That would be impossible. The military does make use of limited conscription when necessary. We've been at war for 20 years, it would be impossible for us to maintain the front otherwise. And while we don't seek to indoctrinate our troops, the war nurtured a bitter hatred towards the enemy and a desperate resolve to prevent our people from having our minds slowly overtaken by the parasites. How could it not?" Said Lillian

    "The situation is similar in our industrial sector. There are times of conflict where we have had to issue state of emergency proclamations and demand around the clock work in shifts beyond normal burdens. Sustaining our workforce with stimulants and mana to keep us from being overwhelmed. Usually this is done voluntarily, but if circumstance prevents that the needs of the nation have mandated forced shifts on rare occasion." Asha explained in an even tone.

    "To expand on the concern of fanaticism I feel I should also express that as head of media part of my role is ensure the spirits of the Aeterian people remain as high as possible. In our minds we are fighting a desperate war of attrition for our very survival. Humanity does it's best when hope and spite coincide. I do play a role in spreading an appropriate amount of exposure to the atrocities done to us during this conflict to maintain our resilient spirit. I do not feel as though anything I've released has been false, I have not had to. However even if the Symbiants had conducted their genocides in a kinder way, I know I'd be willing to paint them as the devil itself if that kept our people determined to fight for their freedom." Andrew Rayleigh continued.

    "The truth of the matter is that this war has taken a toll on my people and the society we maintain. I am unsure of how other species see war, but humanity sees war as an ugly matter. A cruelty that exists out of either failure, or grim necessity. There is a great deal more to the reality of the situation, as my people have already explained. However there is truth behind the hyperbole of the Symbiant propaganda. We are at war Tyr, and war is an ugly, brutal, cruel thing. This war has stolen an entire generation's understanding of peace. The sacrifices and compromises I have had to make to this date, however justified, will never not disgust me. If you wish to look upon aeteria from the lense of a peaceful, progressive nation. You may find yourself disgusted as well. However, you are welcome, and I trust you will look at us and see us as we are within the reality we must live."

    The... the rumors weren't wrong? I must have betrayed the shock with my expression. As the Aeter attempted to soften his expression and continued.

    "I see, I am jealous of your culture, and I am sorry of the burden you will have to carry if you seek to know us properly. However, should you find yourself haunted by what you will come to know of the reality here, you should know you're in good company."
