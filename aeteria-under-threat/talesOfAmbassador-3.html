A tour of Aeterian Life, Day 1

I was duly shocked.

The absolute monarch of an entire dominion of space. One capable of holding itself proud against the Symbiant invasion, lived what I would consider to be the life of a moderately accomplished merchant.

The room wasn't humble, but neither was it unreasonably luxurious. The walls were lined with books. Actual paper books. The kind one might find in museums or on art displays for calligraphy. There were dozens of gems and accessories neatly scattered across the room's furniture, however upon inspection they appeared to be arcane in origin, and were all well worn enough to imply regular use.

Nothing in the room was less than a symbiosis of elegance and comfort. With a melding of styles that implied an appreciation of old and new in equal measure. Where most in positions of power would use and discard items without worry for the waste, as convenience, time, and the imposition of their fiscal might all took precedence the Aeter clearly believed in using only what he valued, and using it for as long as possible before seeking replacement.

"Have you seen enough to your satisfaction Mr. Tyr? It may have been his invitation, but the personal quarters of the Aeter are not a museum for gawking." -

- My guide, Mr Thomas said from behind me where he'd been silently observing me as I explored.

"I am, thank you. Let us continue the tour Mr. Thomas."

The quarters of the other high leadership were a little less surprising, being slightly more extravagant than the example set by the Aeter but were still plenty acetic considering their stations within the Aeterian dominion. None of them had personal quarters exceeding 20 x 20 x 10 feet dimensions.(6 x 6 x 3 meters) with furnishings that came off as practical first, luxurious second.

The inspection of the upper administration had taken the remainder of the day, as well as my energy after such a long journey. The first on my list of priorities for the inspections to come was the military training camps. Followed by military and civilian industrial factories, then hospitals, agriculture, schools and universities, and finally housing. It was an extensive list to go over, and some of my inspections will invariably be redundant enough to only merit note in my findings report, but it was critical to know how humanity found it's treatment under the Aeter.

For now, I was happy to journal my findings and conclusions of today and rest. Little had gone as expected with Aeteria, and my work was looking to be... comprehensive going forward. Tomorrow, before I start the inspections, I should take some time to do an inspection on my entourage. With the militant reputation the Aeterians possessed, the confederation was reluctant to send more people than strictly necessary. After all if safety ever did become an issue no amount of guards would mater deep in Aeterian space. As such my team was comprised of automated crew. Each specialized in their role, and little else. It had made the journey somewhat lonely given their limited personalties but they were reliable and it was better than others dying for the risk.

There were ten of them in all. A pilot, a cook, two maintenance droids, a medic, a salvage drone, and four 'Honor guard' to escort me. In truth there was little to inspect. General maintenance was self administered as long as there remained adequate resources to do so. All logic drives and personality cores came up clean to inspection and all parts appeared and read as in perfect working condition.

It was fortunate that there had proven to be no difficulty with the Aeterians when they'd first met the robotic guards. While we had given advance notice there had been an unspoken worry on our side that given the apparent lack of robotic presence in Aeterian military roles, there may have been a bias against the use of artificial, pre-set, or otherwise non-living warriors. That this was not the case may indicate a simple lack of knowledge in the execution of this technology. That, or perhaps they find it problematic to integrate non-magical divisions into a doctrine that has always assumed magical capacity. With any luck I will find the answer in the coming weeks.

My honor guard and I were picked up for our inspection early the next day, or at least early as far as I was to understand natural human sleep cycles. Cuthians don't require a great deal of rest, however we are also not capable of maintaining high activity for long periods of time either. As a general estimate a Cuthian requires only two hours of inactive mental condition, or 'sleep' to maintain a healthy mental state. Humanity apparently on average needs close to eight hours. However it seems under a commonly available drug known as 'elixir' this requirement is reduced to four without issue.
