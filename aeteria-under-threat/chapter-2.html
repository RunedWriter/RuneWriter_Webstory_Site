    • The Start of a Rocky Relationship

    "I'm sorry, but just to clarify. The rumors, however justified, are true? Or are at the very least based upon reality?"

    A part of me was horrified. I understood of course, that the idea of sacrificing your freedom, and a large element of your own personal will was a horror. As it is to all in the coalition. However, the idea of sacrificing the freedoms, fair treatment, and voices of ones own people simply to protect and maintain the control of a territory, of planets, in SPACE! In the vast cold INFINITE!?! It... it was insane! Why would any species willingly put their own kin through such horrors, over the dirt and rock of mere planets?!? A home is certainly something to defend, but to defend at the cost of self torment? Madness! Before they had a chance to reply, I reined myself in and sought to apologize. It wouldn't due to put the mission at risk over temporary shock.

    "I apologize, I meant to disrespect of course. I am simply surprised for there to be any truth in something spoken by the Symbiant. It has been millennia since a new space faring species has been found and from the perspective of the coalition it is difficult to imagine planetary rigidity being worth the sacrifice of citizen freedoms. Space after all, is infinite, there is always room and material sufficient for comfort if not abundance with the technology known to those able to travel the open wastes between the stars."

    Rayleigh and Lillian both had faint expressions of disapproval registering on my personal Heads up display (HUD), Asha's face registered as aghast when I mentioned the abandonment of planets. Only the Aeter remained neutral, raising a hand to stay the others.

    "Please, let us remember that we are not the only ones with deeply rooted views. Tyr, I believe you are intending to be genuine and gracious in your view. However, to us it is more than simply a place to rest. Before I continue, a question. Are the Cuthians particularly skilled at the arcane arts? It was my understanding after reading the dossier provided by your people that most in your coalition have grown and developed themselves predominantly on the natural laws of the universe with nearly minimal reliance on the arcane. Is this correct?"

    Accepting the grace provided and eager to smooth over my error, I answered openly.

    "You are correct. While of course all living creatures are capable of using the arcane to some extent, it is generally considered a craft less reliable and more demanding than simply learning to build and live in cohesion with the scientific laws of the universe. While a practiced hand will almost never miscast their spell, say statistically one in 10,000 times optimistically. A carefully designed system built on the scientific principals of our universe is never in conflict with the natural law to begin with. Is never up to the interpretation or emotions of it's caster. As such even if it can occasionally lack immediate power we consider it more reliable and scalable for societal use. It is also true that in general from what we have grown to understand that those born on planetary bodies are more naturally inclined to have the abundant mana needed to sustain their arcane practice than those who live within the void."

    "There is a reason for that Tyr, it was to be included amongst the knowledge we intend to share in exchange for the coalitions knowledge on more advanced applications of the natural sciences. However, in the interest of explaining what no doubt seems outrageous to a people used to living in the emptiness. I will share some of that information now.

    The reason you have found those living on planets to have stronger mana and talent for the arcane is that inhabited planets tend to have a higher concentration of spiritual energy in their vicinity. The soul is a living thing, however it doesn't need a living host. Where existence coalesces in the universe, so to does spirit. Even small asteroids will have some mana or spirit within them, but a planet, a giant body of existence, has a great deal more. It may not be sentient or capable of casting arcana as it lacks the will, but there is great mana in a planet and that mana will dwell in those that live and grow upon it.

    We Aeterians are a people of the arcane. Our society is channeled through our understanding of it and the natural sciences in equal measure. To abandon our planets. To lose our homes is not a matter of losing a shelter Tyr, it is a matter of sacrificing a part of our souls. We intend to send a number of ours to live within the coalition as a safeguard against defeat should your people be willing. But please do not ever forget that to us our planets are far, far more than a home. They are our history, they are our heritage, and they are our kin to us. We will fight to our dying breaths to protect them and our people, even if it means great sacrifice."

    The idea of non-sentient bodies holding mana wasn't anathema to the coalition. However, partly due to research of the arcane not being well funded, the idea of inanimate objects contributing to ones own mana growth wasn't something generally considered. Assuming the Aeterians were correct the implications of this meant a great deal. It would affect our approach to the locations of hab stations and training facilities. With more arcane potential there could be schools for mages and progress on attempting to incorporate more reliable arcane material into scientific research. It is unclear if it would be considered consistent enough to integrate fully into societal and military application, but the simple reality of our entire society being stunted in a field planet-born thrive in has been a long time concern of the council.

    It would also neatly explain the symbiant obsession with acquiring the largest of their oppositions planets first, and their practice of concentrating their populations around the largest planetary bodies they can within a given system. We had originally assumed it to be an effort of self evolution, as the larger a planet, the more severe it's gravitational burden. If it is for arcane purposes on the other hand... it is no wonder the Aeterians would be so attached to their terrestrial bodies. That said...

    "Is that why your stations and titans are located in orbit of planetary gas giants most of the time? I had thought the gravity wells were part of the reason, as it would make dimensional travel slightly easier with such a significant landmark."

    "That is part of the reason of course, but yes, where possible we do prefer to have our semi-permanent operations near grand celestial bodies to help maintain the needs of our spirits self-nurturement. You will understand more of the details, once we finalize the exchange of knowledge and technology. To set your mind at ease, would you like a tour of the conditions our people experience in their day to day lives? Perhaps it will not hold to the standards of the coalition, but I personally believe that my administration has truly done their best to preserve the hearts of our people as we bear the burden of this war together."

    "That should prove an amiable compromise for me to work with yes. I have been left with full discretion and authority to decide the coalitions initial stance of course. My only intention is confirm, or rather dissuade the greater concerns of the council. From our view..."

    And here I hoped my impression of the Aeterian's value of honesty and directness would be as true as I believed it to be.

    "... your people are simply too skilled at warfare. It scares us. No one, not a single species before you has weathered the Symbiant threat after they'd grown to power. You are new to the galactic stage. You are a young species, surrounded by stories of violence, totalitarianism, and expansionism. You are so much more powerful than anyone would expect another species in your shoes to be.

    The truth is we hate the Symbiants. They stole our homes, they've forced us to live as nomads in the darkness of space. They take the will of all those they encounter with honeyed lies and false paradise. I would like to trust you Aeter, I would like to trust your people. But my people need to make sure we do not replace one despotic power with another.

    It will be my honor to accept your offer, to see the lives your people live on all levels of your society. And should it prove as understandable as you have led me to believe I will rejoice in the partnership of our people. However if I see a people oppressed without cause, or believe myself to be hidden from the truth of their experience I will be forced to advise the coalition to oppose you and your government as vehemently as we do the Symbiant. Humanity has already convinced me of their capacity for honor and valor. I am sure we both agree they are deserving of respect and freedom wherever possible."

    My heart raced, and my tendrils itched uncomfortably. I had never been so blunt and callous on a first meeting with a prospective ally. It felt so, so wrong. But my gut told me that the warrior blood of these humans, and the straightforwardness of their leader demanded a steeled approach to negotiation if I was to earn their respect. With a silent, internal sigh, my HUD showed positive reactions from their delegation. It even highlighted the faint 'smile' that quickly showed, then disappeared from the Aeter's face.

    "You do your people credit Tyr. I wish I could give you the tour personally, but I must begin preparations to return to the front. I'll will permit your access to all non-highly classified institutions. Please inform my colleagues of your priorities for inspection and you will be assigned guides to tour them across the upcoming days. I am sure you understand that until our next planned diplomatic meeting, we will be occupied with our duties. However, once you have finished here and your planned tours are scheduled. I implore you to inspect the personal quarters of myself and the upper administration. I believe it will prove beneficial in your evaluation on the standards and treatment found across the tiers of Aeterian society."

    "Thank you, your majesty. I will do just that."
